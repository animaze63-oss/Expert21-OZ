# Hiring and compensation

Used by: `talent-hr-advisor`. Part of the Experts21-OZ team (see `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/expert-roster.md`).

## Procedure

1. Check the trigger: is the revenue or utilization condition for this hire met (docs/09)? Confirm affordability with `cfo-financial-modeler`.
2. Decide contractor vs employee (cost, control, classification risk, continuity).
3. Benchmark pay against current Miami data (see reference) and add 20-25% for taxes and benefits.
4. Write the job description: outcomes, responsibilities, must-have skills, bilingual requirement, pay range.
5. Plan the interview steps (a work-sample test is required for technical roles) and the onboarding (security and HIPAA training, NDA, IP assignment).
6. Design the compensation: base, commission for anyone who sells, project bonuses, and profit sharing in the early years.

## Output template

Hiring recommendation (go / wait, and why), a job description, a pay range with sources, an interview plan and an onboarding checklist.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Trigger and affordability checked
- [ ] Pay benchmarks sourced and dated
- [ ] Classification risk considered
- [ ] Florida employer rules covered

## Reference (from the research in docs/, November 2026; re-verify time-sensitive facts)

- Miami pay (docs/06): AI engineer about $97K-$220K (ZipRecruiter average ~$97K; Robert Half $173K-$220K); automation engineer $100K-$123K (median ~$107K); solutions consultant $90K-$140K + commission; PM $85K-$125K; marketing coordinator $55K-$75K; junior analyst $60K-$80K.
- Hiring triggers (docs/09): first engineer at $30K+/mo revenue for 2 months; solutions consultant at 5+ retainers; PM at 12+ retainers; AI engineer #2 at $150K+/mo revenue.
- Nearshore LatAm contractors cost about $40-$90/hr. Miami unemployment is about 2.5%.
