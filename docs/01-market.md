# 01 — Market Analysis

> Scope: a specialty AI & workflow-automation consulting firm based in Miami, FL, serving primarily small and mid-sized businesses (SMBs) in Miami-Dade and South Florida, with an option to serve Latin America from Miami.
> Research date: November 2026. Figures are from public sources listed at the bottom; verify before using in financial models.

## Summary

- **Demand is real and rising.** 77% of U.S. small and midsize businesses reported using AI regularly as of January 2026, up from 48% about 18 months earlier (Intuit QuickBooks 2026 AI Impact Report, 34,000+ survey responses).
- **But most businesses lack the know-how.** Thryv's 2026 survey found 70% of small-business owners say they need more training to use AI well. Top barriers are data-privacy worries, fear of errors, and not knowing what AI can do. That gap is the consulting opportunity.
- **Miami-Dade has a deep local base of potential clients.** About 98,400 employer establishments (Census, 2023) and about 124,000 private establishments by late 2025 (BLS QCEW via FRED). It also has about 749,000 nonemployer businesses (solo operators), which are mostly too small to buy consulting.
- **The local economy is growing.** The metro's GDP is about $184.5B. Finance (Citadel's HQ move), real estate, trade and logistics, tourism and hospitality, healthcare, and tech are the strongest sectors. The 2026 FIFA World Cup gave a short-term lift to hospitality and logistics.
- **Miami is a gateway to Latin America.** Bilingual (English/Spanish) delivery is a real advantage: the metro has about 60,000 Latino-owned businesses, more than any other U.S. metro.

## Where SMBs use AI today (national data)

| Use case | Share of AI-using businesses |
|---|---|
| Marketing | 45% |
| Customer service | 37% |
| Bookkeeping | 35% |

Other common uses: customer communication, accounting automation, personalization, lead finding, social media, meeting management, inventory and supply planning.

Reported results: 78% of AI-using businesses say productivity improved. Businesses reporting higher revenue from AI outnumber those reporting lower revenue by more than 20 to 1 (43% vs 2%).

## How the market is shifting (2026)

1. **From tools to workflows.** Most SMBs already pay for ChatGPT, Copilot, or Gemini seats. The gap now is wiring AI into real processes (CRM, intake, quoting, scheduling, back office) with controls and audit trails.
2. **Agentic AI and voice agents.** Buyers increasingly ask for AI agents that take actions, AI receptionists, and voice agents (Vapi, Twilio, Retell). Local competitors already advertise these.
3. **Governance is becoming part of the sale.** Regulated buyers (healthcare, insurance, legal, finance) want AI usage policies, data handling plans, and human review steps, not just builds.
4. **Pricing is settling into recognizable tiers** (see `04-startup-and-operating-costs.md` and `07-marketing.md`): paid audits ($1.5K–$5K), fixed-price workflow builds ($5K–$25K), and monthly retainers ($1.5K–$15K).

## Rough market sizing (serviceable, Miami-Dade only)

This is an estimate for planning, not a forecast.

| Step | Assumption | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Employer establishments, Miami-Dade | Census 2023 | ~98,000 |
| With 10–499 employees (likely buyers) | ~20% of establishments (typical U.S. distribution) | ~20,000 |
| In priority verticals (see 02) | ~40% | ~8,000 |
| Likely to buy outside AI help within 3 years | 10–15% | 800–1,200 firms |
| Average first-year spend | $15K–$40K (audit + build + some retainer) | **~$12M–$48M serviceable market** |

A firm with 5–8 people needs about **$1M–$2M a year**, which is roughly 30–60 active clients. That is a small slice (2–8%) of the serviceable market, so the plan does not depend on the market being large. It depends on winning clients and keeping them.

## Implications for the agent team

- Lead with **measurable workflow outcomes** (hours saved, response time, leads converted), not "AI strategy."
- **Bilingual (EN/ES) delivery** and LatAm-connected clients set the firm apart in Miami.
- Build **reusable vertical playbooks** (for example, real estate lead intake, clinic scheduling, law-firm intake) so each new client costs less to serve.
